Oriel Foundation (明窗基金會) is a foundation in formation for families in need and their children. An oriel is a small window that juts out from a wall, and the name stands for one simple aim: open a window of light for a child.

Its founder, Scott Park, says he started Oriel after volunteering in Thailand in 2020 and in Congo in 2025. He is also the founder of CHAINBRIEF, and this piece is published as partner content with that relationship disclosed.

## At a glance

- **Learn** — tutoring, books and scholarships with local teachers.
- **Live** — meals, clean water, health checks, safe travel to school and living support for struggling families.
- **Build** — classrooms, libraries and schools, only when a community asks, and only with people Oriel already works with.

## Why it isn't taking donations yet

Oriel is writing its charter and preparing to register as a nonprofit. Until that is done, the foundation says it will not publish a bank account or wallet address or accept any gift. Once giving opens, it plans to put every transaction on a public ledger anyone can check.

Its website also commits to using children's photos only with a guardian's written consent, and never publishing names or places that could identify a child.

## What comes next

According to its roadmap, Oriel aims to set its charter, advisers and first local partner by December, launch publicly in January 2027, and start a first program with one community and one partner during 2027. People and organisations who want to help can reach it through the Get involved page or by email.
