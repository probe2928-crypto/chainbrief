MIURO is an AI music company built on its own foundation model. Its pitch is about what happens after the song is made: instead of a loose audio file, each release becomes a work with a clear author, version history and licence. Creators talk to an agent called Claw to make and publish a track, and the company says identity, rights and settlement are handled in the background.

## At a glance

- **GM-1** — the in-house music foundation model. Lyrics, vocals, melody and arrangement come out of one system. The company says it is strongest in Mandarin.
- **Miuro Claw** — a conversational agent to create, edit, split stems and publish.
- **Work layer** — every release gets a work identity, a version record and a licence scope.
- **Pricing** — membership, pay-as-you-go credits, and a packaged music API for businesses.

## What sets it apart

There is no shortage of AI music generators. MIURO's argument is about ownership: a published track arrives with a version record and a defined commercial scope. The blockchain sits underneath for ownership and settlement, the company says, while the product itself looks and works like a music app.

According to the official site, generated songs include a commercial licence, and higher tiers add exclusive rights and royalty NFT minting. Music NFTs are supported on Ethereum, Solana and Base.

## Recent: Pudgy Beats

MIURO is working with Pudgy Penguins on Pudgy Beats. Fans generate in Claw and publish the result as a work, with rewards tied to creation rather than to running a node, as the company describes it.

## Try it

New accounts get 100 free credits, roughly ten songs, with no card required, according to the site. Partnership enquiries go to the email and Telegram contacts in the fact sheet.
