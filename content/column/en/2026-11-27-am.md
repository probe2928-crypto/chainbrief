---
title: Bitcoin is back above $60,000. The new buyer is not
time: 2026-11-27T12:47:00+09:00
---
Bitcoin climbed back above $60,000 in two days. Once roughly $7 billion of options expire at 08:00 UTC today, who is left to hold the price up?

Start with how it got there. Bitcoin slid into the $57,000s on Nov. 25, then touched about $61,500 on Thanksgiving. CoinGlass data show about $264 million of bitcoin futures liquidated in that 24 hours, with shorts ($143 million) outpacing longs ($121 million). Traders who bet on a fall were forced to buy back, and that buying lifted the price.

Spot money moved the other way. Farside data show U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost $453 million on Nov. 25, about $330 million of it from BlackRock's IBIT. That was the seventh straight day of outflows, and November's $5.39 billion so far is the largest monthly outflow since the funds launched.

Together with about $600 million of ether options, today's expiry is about a quarter of Deribit's open options book. The bitcoin put-call ratio is 0.89, up from 0.55 in late October, with puts stacked at $60,000, $58,000, $50,000 and $40,000. Deribit's DVOL volatility index sits near 55, against about 38 in October.

Readings differ. Deribit chief commercial officer Jean-David Pequignot describes most of those puts as insurance bought by holders of spot or futures, not a forecast. Bitfinex research, in a Nov. 25 note, found recent buyers were mostly leveraged futures traders rather than spot accumulators. Trader Ted Pillows called the bounce forced short covering, not new money.

As I see it, both can be true at once. Hedged holders are not selling, and squeezed shorts have no reason to buy again. Neither is a new owner. A rally powered by liquidations stops when the shorts run out.

The first test arrives with Friday's half-day ETF flows, reported early Saturday in Asia: an eighth day of outflows, or the first pause.
