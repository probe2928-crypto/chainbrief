---
title: The December hike is priced. The size isn't.
time: 2026-11-26T18:48:00+09:00
---
Wall Street is closed for Thanksgiving, but bitcoin trades on. If a Federal Reserve rate hike on December 9 already looks close to certain, what exactly is the crypto market still afraid of?

Start with what is confirmed. The Commerce Department's core PCE index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, rose 3.3% year on year in October, above the 3.2% consensus and September's 3.1%. Bitcoin slid to around $57,500 after the release. US spot bitcoin ETFs logged a sixth straight day of net outflows on November 24, per SoSoValue data. And this afternoon in Seoul, the Bank of Korea lifted its base rate to 3.25%.

Readings differ on what comes next. Nick Forster, founder of options venue Derive, said mid-month that the Fed could move by 25 or even 50 basis points, noting that downside protection was costing more than equivalent upside exposure — a sign traders had turned defensive. Tickmill's Ranim Turfa put it more simply: bitcoin's near-term direction will hinge on macro data. Few dispute that a hike is in the price. The open question is how big, and how many.

My read: the pressure isn't the hike itself but the uncertainty around its size and sequel. A quarter point is old news. If pricing drifts toward 50 basis points or a January follow-up, expect another leg of stress. If data cools, one well-telegraphed hike could actually clear the air. Thin holiday liquidity today and Friday's half session can exaggerate either move.

The last big input before the decision is the November jobs report on December 4 at 8:30 a.m. ET. The Fed meets December 8–9, and November CPI lands the day after, on December 10.
